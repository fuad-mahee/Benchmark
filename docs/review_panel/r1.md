## (a) Overall assessment

The study's core contributions are real and, where I could check them, numerically exact: the census counts (988 / 2,552), the anchoring to GlitchCleaner's published list (2,537 of 2,539, Jaccard 0.9933), the α/β grid marginals, the kernel-divergence rates (0.37%/0.42% text, 2 of 9,522 verdicts), the false-positive counts, and the newly added five-model rescaling table all reproduce from the artefacts. The epistemics section (`sec:epistemics`), the anchored/not-anchored split, and the self-audit chapter are genuinely above the norm for reproduction work, and the retained raw generations made independent verification possible — I recomputed the entire RQ1 factorial from stored data without re-running the model. However, the document repeatedly states conclusions at a strength its three seeds and single-seed grid cannot carry, and one headline table caption is **falsified by the study's own archived data**: the factorial's no-interaction claim is false, and the interaction is larger than the main effect it attaches to. Several comparisons framed as single-variable controls change three variables at once — including one that violates the denominator rule the study exists to enforce.

**2. Line 663: "so the base rate explains part of the gap" — unsupported at n=3, and it is not a base-rate control.**

Two independent problems.

*Statistical.* The two conditions use identical seed samples (I verified: the `label_sample_seed{0,1,2}.csv` token-id sets are byte-identical between `checkpoints/` and `gccode/checkpoints/`), so this is a paired design and should be analysed as one. Paired differences are 0.239, 0.047, 0.089: mean +0.125, sd 0.101, **t(2) = 2.148, p = 0.165, 95% CI [−0.125, +0.375]**. The interval contains zero and contains the whole effect twice over. Three seeds cannot license "explains part of the gap."

*Design.* This is item (5) of my brief. Moving from $P_{\mathrm{paper}}$ to $P_{\mathrm{code}}$ changes the prompt wording, the generation budget, **and** the match rule simultaneously — and by your own Section `sec:rq1`, the dominant changed variable is prompt wording, not base rate. Worse, the labelling function itself changes: 146 tokens flip glitch→normal and 1,710 flip normal→glitch, so the classifier is not learning a denser version of the same concept but a different concept, one where 42.7% of positives are a newline-attractor artefact. Calling this "the base-rate control" gives a single-variable name to a three-variable change.

**Required fix.** Replace lines 658–665 with:

> "Under $P_{\mathrm{paper}}$ recall is $0.279$ (95% CI [0.053, 0.505], three seeds). One might suspect this is depressed because our stricter census makes positives rarer. Re-scoring under $P_{\mathrm{code}}$, whose glitch base rate (8.0%) matches the conditions the papers worked in, gives $0.404$ (95% CI [0.357, 0.451]). The paired difference across the three shared seeds is $+0.125$ (95% CI $[-0.125, +0.375]$, $t(2)=2.148$, $p=0.17$): with three seeds we cannot establish that the protocol change raises recall at all. What we can state is the conclusion that matters: under either protocol the claimed $0.674$ lies outside the measured interval, decisively so under $P_{\mathrm{code}}$. We also note that this contrast is not a clean base-rate manipulation — the two protocols differ in prompt wording, generation budget and match rule, and 146 tokens change class in the opposite direction, so the target concept differs and not merely its prevalence."

Retitle the paragraph "Recall does not reproduce under either protocol" (the current title asserts the causal conclusion you should be withdrawing). If you want a real base-rate control, the single-variable version is to hold the census fixed and vary $\gamma$ — that changes positives-in-sample without changing the labelling function, and it is cheap.

## (c) Optional improvements

- **Line 405** ("Every sampled quantity is run at three seeds and reported as mean ± sample standard deviation... Three seeds bound variance only loosely") is honest and well-placed, but it should say what will be *done* about it: state that all interval estimates use $t_{0.975,2} = 4.303$ and that no claim rests on a difference whose CI includes zero. Then hold to it — items 2, 3 and 6 above are where it is currently not held to.
- **Recall is partly definitional and this is never decomposed.** Roughly $\gamma = 10\%$ of $|G|$ enters the predicted set for free via brute-force sample labelling. Per seed the free component is 86/112/99 ($P_{\mathrm{paper}}$) and 258/266/277 ($P_{\mathrm{code}}$), so the classifier's *own* contribution to $P_{\mathrm{paper}}$ recall ranges from 8.7% (seed 0) to 22.2% (seed 1) — a 2.5× spread that the headline recall number compresses. Reporting "recall, and recall net of the labelled sample" would sharpen RQ2 considerably at zero compute cost, and it is a fairer characterisation of the detector than the pooled figure both papers use.
- **`m` sweep, line 811.** "the selection changes by an order of magnitude (376 promoted and 530 suppressed neurons in total, against the counts at $m = 1$)" never gives the $m = 1$ counts, so the reader cannot evaluate it. From `alpha_beta_grid_meta.json` they are **63 promoted / 17,041 suppressed**. Note that the two move in *opposite* directions (promotion ×6 up, suppression ×32 down), which "changes by an order of magnitude" obscures. `m_sweep.csv` also already records repair_rate 0.05 at $m = 0.25$ against ~20.8% at $m = 1$ — a large effect that is in the artefact but not the text.
- **Section 1.3, item 1.** The three derivations of 2,779 are arithmetically sound but not independent (all from one paper's tables, all inverting rounded rates). Worth one clause: the rounding permits 2,778–2,780, which does not affect the argument.
- **Threats / internal validity.** GlitchCleaner at one adapter seed is acknowledged. Consider stating the consequence explicitly: no variance claim about GlitchCleaner is possible at all, so the train-vs-heldout gap (a within-run paired contrast) is the only quantity that can be defended from a single seed — which happens to be the quantity you rely on, so say so.
- The `\Neunup` / `\Neundown` macros (lines 28–29) are defined but never used.

## (d) Verdict

**Major revisions.** The study's substantive findings — protocol-dependence of the census, the memorisation gap, the unrecoverable calibration, the rescaled competitor counts — are sound and in several places verified exactly against the artefacts; but a headline table caption is falsified by the study's own data, two RQ2 claims are stated at strengths three seeds cannot support, one table's only completed contrast is confounded three ways, the α/β section contradicts its own stated noise standard, and the strongest GlitchProber claim routes through a cross-denominator comparison the study exists to forbid. Almost all of this is fixable from data already on disk without new compute, which is why the verdict is revisions rather than reject — but the volume and the presence of a demonstrably false claim in a headline table put it past "accept with revisions."
